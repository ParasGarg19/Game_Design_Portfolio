Systems & Narrative Design Case Study

Ashes Of Truth

Structuring investigative tension, 3D exploration limits and level pacing in a PC narrative thriller.

Project Overview

Ashes of Truth is a first-person narrative thriller focusing on a junior news investigator uncovering a deep corporate conspiracy surrounding a burned mansion. The game was designed for the theme of "Women Empowerment"—defining strength not through physical combat dominance, but through curiosity, investigative determination and moral courage.

Rather than building an action-oriented game, our team focused on creating a grounded thriller. The player must navigate discrete locations, interrogate suspects, compile profiles and escape hostile ambush encounters while protecting their gathered evidence.

The Design Challenge

How do we design a compelling investigative thriller without resorting to traditional combat or puzzle placeholders? We had to create mechanics that make the player feel like an active detective—uncovering leads naturally—while working within a tight one week development timeframe that restricted our ability to build a fully realized 3D city.

Design Goals

  • Empower Through Intellect: Establish an investigative core loop where players connect clues to suspect profiles.
  • Strict Scoping: Design a hybrid 2D/3D structure to bypass the engineering cost of an open-world navigation system.
  • Paced Narrative Escalation: Map a narrative arc across different level scenes, moving from safe office routines to tense stealth confrontations.
  • Survival-Oriented Evasion: Combat must feel threatening, forcing evasion, hiding and quick thinking rather than confrontation.

Core Systems

1. 2D Travel Map

To bypass building large travel zones, we created a 2D fast-travel interface. Players select discovered locations (News Office, Mansion, Slum, Forest Cabin) on a retro-styled vector map. This kept focus entirely on high-detail 3D environmental spaces.

2. Suspect Profiler Board

Collected clues are logged into a suspect database. The player manually links clues (e.g. *burned note fragments*) to specific suspect profiles (*The Businessman, The Mercenary*). This visual feedback loop rewards players for drawing connections.

3. Survival Evasion

When gangsters ambush the forest hideout (Scene 10) and final mansion return (Scene 16), the gameplay shifts to high-tension evasion. The player has no weapons. They must use environmental layouts—ducking behind crates, utilizing darkness and timing escapes—to survive.

Design Decisions & Scope Management

Managing scope was critical to releasing the prototype on time. Here is how we adjusted our design:

Original Design Concept Scoped Iteration Design Value / Impact
Open World Town Map 2D Interactive Map Interface Saved 60% of art/level production time, allowing us to focus on 3D mansion details.
Dynamic Gunplay Combat Stealth Evasion & Evasion Reinforced the thematic hook of a vulnerable protagonist using wits over power.
Complex Physics Puzzles Dialogue-Driven Clue Triggers Aligned mechanics with investigative gameplay and reduced programming dependency.

Problems Faced

Clue Hunting Fatigue

During early tests, players complained that searching the 3D mansion for clues felt like pixel-hunting. Since evidence matched the desaturated environment aesthetic, players walked right past critical case files.

Pacing Drops during Dialogue

Long, non-interactive text boxes slowed down the pacing during slums investigation scenes, breaking the tension built up during earlier stages.

Design Iterations

Iteration 1: Hidden Clues

Clues were placed naturally in drawers and desks without any highlights, relying entirely on raw exploration.

Result: Playtesters felt frustrated, spending up to 20 minutes search-clicking single rooms.

Iteration 2: Subtle Shimmer Highlight

Added a subtle glowing dust shader (shimmer effect) that triggers when the player gets within 3 meters of evidence, paired with a soft UI sound cue.

Result: Success. Maintained search immersion while eliminating player search fatigue.

Resolving Pacing: Key Choice Dialogue

To make dialogue engaging, we iterated the conversation structure. Dialogues were converted into short suspect-interrogation blocks where the player chooses question categories (e.g. *Mansion Fire, Alibi, Suspect connections*). Selecting categories dynamically unlocks profile updates.

Playtesting Outcomes

Playtesting sessions conducted with internal developers yielded the following feedback:

  • Clue Findability: Adding the shader shimmer highlights improved the clue retrieval rate by 70%.
  • Engagement: 85% of players successfully linked evidence to the correct profiles on their first attempt, confirming UI board clarity.
  • Ambush Evasion: Evasion pacing was balanced after adding warning audio cues (e.g. doors crashing open) 3 seconds before gangsters enter the space.

Lessons Learned

Affordance is Critical: In exploration games, the boundary between "immersive searching" and "tedious hunting" is thin. Designers must provide clear visual and auditory affordances (highlights, sound cues) to keep players focused on making deductions rather than fighting the camera interface.

Final Outcome

The vertical slice successfully demonstrated Level 1 (Office introduction) and Level 2 (First Mansion exploration). By keeping a tight scope with our hybrid 2D/3D structure, we delivered a polished prototype highlighting our team's core narrative design, pacing structure and systems balancing capability.
