Systems & Hybrid Design Case Study

The Last Resistance AR

Adapting physical tabletop board game asymmetry into a marker-based Mobile AR companion experience.

Project Overview

The Last Resistance is a narrative-driven, asymmetrical strategy board game set in an alternate Cold War scenario. In the tabletop version, one player commands the Soviet occupation force—wielding strongholds, reinforcements and long-range artillery—while three other players coordinate as Rebel cells attempting to liberate their city.

The AR Adaptation merges the physical board game with a marker-based mobile app. The goal is to offload administrative systems—specifically hidden player roles, real-time morale variables and artillery targeting overlays—into the digital space, allowing players to focus entirely on social dynamics, coordination and tabletop strategy.

The Design Challenge

How do we integrate Augmented Reality into a physical board game without breaking the "tabletop magic"? Playtesting indicated that full digitization turned a highly interactive, face-to-face strategy game into a disconnected screen-staring experience. The challenge lay in establishing a design framework for selective digitization—converting only systems that benefit from real-time simulation and keeping physical elements tactile.

Design Goals

  • Preserve Board Tactility: The map, tokens and movement must remain physical. AR should act as an overlay, not a replacement.
  • Minimize Administrative Friction: Automate hidden cards, variable calculation and turns. Reduce game-state tracking time.
  • Enhance Psychological Tension: Use private digital UI to manage hidden identities and betrayal states without human error or leakage.
  • Improve Visual Feedback: Project real-time tactical overlays (e.g. Soviet artillery threat zones) onto the board to build anticipation.

Core Systems

1. Dynamic Morale System

Rebels operate on a Morale scale (0–10). In the board game, tracking this manually with cardboard slides felt tedious and lacked emotional impact. In AR, floating desaturated meters are projected directly above each player's token. The meter's color state (cyan, purple, critical red) shifts instantly in response to events, turning morale into a live system players actively watch.

2. The Hidden Betrayal System

One Rebel role card secretly contains the "Corruptible" trait. When a specific stronghold—*Tatarin's Command Outpost*—is captured, a hidden trigger fires. The AR app evaluates who holds the card and privately pushes an offer: "Switch sides and join the Soviet Leader." The AR app handles this transition silently, hiding the new Soviet Ally's identity (*General Bulba*) until they decide to deploy their hidden strength.

3. Soviet Artillery Warning HUD

Every three rounds, the Soviet Leader can call in shelling. Rather than rolling dice on target charts, the AR app projects a pulsing red warning grid onto the targeted board spaces. This forces Rebels in those zones to make an immediate tactical choice: spend resources to escape during their turn, or brace for morale damage.

Design Decisions: Physical vs. Digital

To maintain gameplay balance, systems were separated into three clear layers:

System Classification Game Element Reasoning
Physical Layer Board Layout, Token Movement, Combat Dice Maintains social presence, spatial orientation, and tactile satisfaction.
AR Digital Layer Morale Bars, Secret Identities, Card Decks, Target Zones Automates math, prevents accidental information leaks, and displays visual hazards.
Hybrid Integration Dice roll outcomes evaluated by camera feed Saves physical roll satisfaction while automating outcome calculation.

Problems Faced

The "Phone Passing" Loophole

Originally, event cards were drawn from a single shared phone. This completely ruined the secret role mechanic because other players could read visual cues or notice how long a player spent looking at a screen.

Marker Occlusion

During intense moves, players' hands repeatedly blocked the board markers, causing AR tracking to break and causing UI drift.

Design Iterations

Iteration 1: Shared Mobile Hub

A single mobile device sat beside the board. Players passed it to draw event cards and update stats.

Result: Playtesting failed. Passing the phone took players out of the board experience and created massive down-time.

Iteration 2: Local Network Sync

Moved to a synchronized system where each player scanned the board with their own device. Private information (roles, traits) was sent exclusively to that player's screen.

Result: Success. Secrecy was maintained, and floating stats updated in real-time across all views.

Resolving Occlusion: Board Anchoring

To resolve tracking loss, we abandoned tracking individual tokens. Instead, the AR system anchors onto a single primary board marker. The software calculates offsets for all spaces on the layout. Even if a player blocks a token, the coordinate coordinates are preserved.

Playtesting & Performance Metrics

Playtesting sessions conducted over a 2-week period with diverse gamer profiles yielded the following data:

  • Game Duration reduction: Average match time dropped from 75 minutes (manually calculating event text and meters) to 42 minutes.
  • Rules Verification: Instantly evaluating legal spaces for Rebel movements dropped rulebook checking disputes to zero.
  • Betrayal Engagement: 90% of players reported higher immersion during the hidden betrayal trigger, as they didn't have to announce anything physically.

Lessons Learned

AR is a Supporting Actor: Augmented Reality works best when it operates as an invisible game master. When design forces the player to stare at a screen for more than 15 seconds during a tabletop game, the social layer breaks down. Keep overlays visual, fast, and responsive.

Final Outcome

The hybrid board design successfully balanced asymmetrical mechanics with digital automation. The project demonstrates an advanced level design and systems framework—proving that hardware tracking and digital UI can enhance traditional, face-to-face social strategy games.
